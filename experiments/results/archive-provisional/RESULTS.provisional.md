## Reading the results first: the baseline is weak, so most comparisons tie

With library defaults the rules extractor recovers a small share of the truth claims (all-type recall: demo 0.240 (6/25), train 0.213 (10/47), validation 0.182 (4/22)) and few outcome claims (outcome recall: demo 0.000 (0/4), train 0.222 (2/9), validation 0.250 (1/4)), because the families paraphrase ("Both sessions are up", "The session is steady now") outside the rule lexicons. In E00, 21/24 investigations end in the recommendation `none`, and 14/15 questions asked were answered "unknown" by their recipient. Comparisons that act on downstream recommendations (E02 downstream columns, E04, E05, E06) therefore tie at zero or near zero. The frozen rules were applied mechanically and the ties are reported as ties; they are evidence that **extraction coverage, not retrieval or selection, is the binding constraint on these families**, not evidence that the variants are equivalent. E01 (below) is the experiment that could change this: it scores a recorded Claude-agent extraction arm on the same train and validation families.

## What the results do NOT show

- **No real engineer benefit.** All people are simulated by an oracle that reads a hidden answer key authored by one agent in one pass, unreviewed (`data/scenarios/REVIEW.md`). Nothing here measures whether an engineer is helped (U04).
- **Synthetic, small, single-author data.** Ten families (demo 2, train 4, validation 2, test 2), 30 problems; the validation and test splits have 6 problems each, so one problem moves a rate by 0.17 or more. Shared phrasing across families may flatter learned components.
- **Ties are not equivalence.** With a baseline that rarely issues advice or asks a useful question, E02 downstream columns, E04, E05 and E06 cannot separate their variants.
- **The simulator advantage of LinUCB is by construction.** Benefit in the simulator depends on the same visible features the bandit sees; it shows the model can learn them, not that it helps with real people.
- **The family arm cannot train a useful bandit.** Only a handful of labelled questions exist per seed (unanswered ones are skipped, never 0), and LinUCB is deterministic: the 3 seeds only reorder the training families.
- **No learned retrieval.** The "local" vector route is hashed character trigrams, not a trained encoder. The usefulness classifier is a small local model on synthetic labels and is not part of the application.
- **No time-to-decision, no token or call counts** were measured (null, never 0).

## Harness limitations

- Outcome questions ("Did traffic return after ...?") are free text; the harness translates them into recovery-state condition keys (`traffic.state`, `bgp.session`, `alarms.state`, `temperature.state`, `interface.status`) before the oracle answers. The oracle is therefore somewhat more forgiving than a real person.
- An unavailable or non-answering recipient is modelled by shelving (`defer`) the question so another person can be tried; the evaluator counts it as unanswered.
- Time is simulated: each problem is on its own day (budgets are per day) unless an experiment sets a spacing; each answer advances 5 minutes, so a 30 minute cooldown blocks a second question to the same person inside one investigation.
- `importFromPath(scenario-dir)` reads sources only and ignores `truth.json`/`incoming.json`; the family folder path is passed to it. Hidden keys are read by the harness only for the oracle and for scoring.
- `lookalike-different-cause` is a family tag; the per-problem list comes from the evaluation owner's tag table in `data/scenarios/REVIEW.md` (the sealed test family uses all of its problems).
- `mergeReports` sums counts across families, so latency percentiles are recomputed from per-problem latencies.
- Code under `src/` is edited by other workers during the night; results come from the working tree at the time of the run.
