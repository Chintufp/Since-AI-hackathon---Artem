# Pitch film — director and animation brief

**Creative direction · 6 November 2026 · Film production is planned, not completed**

Artem wants a cinematic, visually ambitious presentation with multiple screens, animated relationships, coherent incident histories and visible learning loops. A director/animation agent will develop it with him. Read [the product brief](BRIEF.md), [the experience brief](EXPERIENCE_BRIEF.md), [the architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md) and [the master map](master-map.html).

## The story

> Knowledge already exists across the organization. We connect the fragments, preserve what they mean, and make them useful at the next decision—for an engineer or an autonomous agent.

Tell the four problems in the product brief through one visual journey. The viewer should understand both the scale of scattered knowledge and the simplicity of the engineer's interaction. The video is a planned pitch asset alongside the product demonstration.

## Visual ambition

- Begin with several screens or floating windows: an old email, a specialist chat, a local note, a screenshot and an incident view.
- Use source passages as the visual material that becomes claims; avoid a graph that appears from nowhere.
- Move from fragmented screens into an organized topology: evidence, equipment, incidents, hypotheses, attempted responses, outcomes and people.
- Keep a stable visual grammar for observed facts, hypotheses, conflicts, procedures and people so the graph can become complex without becoming arbitrary.
- Show a focused path through the graph when a new incident arrives: matching conditions light up, a conflicting branch remains distinct, and a useful next check emerges.
- Cut from the large topology to the small avatar interaction: one engineer answers one precise question.
- Let the answer visibly update the relevant relationship, then show that information reused elsewhere. A separate learning sequence can show logged decisions, evaluation and a later policy version.
- Include the relevant people: bring a contributor forward from the graph with an explanation of their connection to the incident.
- End with the same service reaching an engineer interface and an agent through MCP, followed by an intended macOS/Windows experience shot.

## Draft sequence for the director

These beats are a starting storyboard, not locked timing or final art direction. A short main cut and an extended cut are useful options; choose duration around the actual pitch slot.

| Beat | On-screen scene | Meaning / narration direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Scattered experience | Multiple screens, overlapping conversations and an unresolved operational problem. | The missing detail already exists, but the workflow cannot find it. |
| 2. Context becomes knowledge | Selected source passages form linked claims while irrelevant chatter recedes. | Capture useful context from existing work. |
| 3. History becomes coherent | An equipment node connects to one incident, attempts, a temporary recovery and a later outcome. Related incidents sit nearby without merging into one event. | Preserve what happened, who contributed and which conditions matter. |
| 4. Similarity becomes a question | A new incident activates two candidate paths; predicted traits appear beside them, and a mismatch dims one path. | Test whether the earlier experience applies now. |
| 5. A tiny contribution | The avatar presents one contextual Yes/No question; a permitted answer updates a particular edge. | Ask only for a useful missing detail. |
| 6. Reward and human connection | Optional pixel celebration/point increment, then a relevant-person card opens into a case contribution. | Make contributing enjoyable and help people find colleagues with relevant experience. |
| 7. Reuse and learning | The updated knowledge helps another incident; decision/outcome records feed a separate evaluation-and-policy-update loop. | Memory updates now; learned selection improves through evaluated releases. |
| 8. Autonomous access and closing | MCP context arrives in an agent's workflow; the companion expands to show the history; platform and organization-hosting shots conclude. | One shared knowledge service supports engineers and agents. |

## Topology and learning details worth showing

- Multiple sources can describe the same event. Visually joining them should not imply several independent confirmations.
- Cases have branches and conditions. A failed attempt and a temporary workaround remain visible parts of the history.
- A person connects through a specific contribution: observed, tested, proposed, reported or confirmed under the chosen policy.
- Distinguish support and disagreement from mere semantic similarity. A graph edge should have an understandable meaning.
- A new answer may update memory immediately. A policy-training montage represents a separate process with evaluation before release.
- The model's question choice includes choosing whom to ask, when to ask and when no interruption is worthwhile.

Use cinematic metaphor freely while keeping these product meanings consistent. Mark simulated scenes, concept animation and measured implementation footage appropriately; a topology animation itself does not establish a trained policy or performance result. Avoid invented accuracy counters.

## Rewards-on and rewards-off versions

Prepare the contribution scene so its point effects and sounds can be enabled or removed without recutting the story. The quiet version still shows the engineer's answer and its useful consequence. Coordinate assets and sound with the experience agent.

## Handoff deliverables

1. Discuss Artem's visual references, pacing, color, mood and camera ideas.
2. Produce a storyboard and animatic before final rendering.
3. Define the graph visual grammar and the screen/window layout for each beat.
4. Draft narration and short on-screen captions around the four problems.
5. List required application captures, mockups, synthetic source text and avatar assets.
6. Produce layered/editable project files, clean export and subtitles/captions; exact tooling and format remain open.
7. Export a main cut, a short fallback cut, and rewards-on/off variants if the chosen edit supports them.

Use synthetic or authorized material in visible screens. The agent should not access accounts, collect real conversations or publish the film merely because this production brief exists.
