# Compact companion revision — 6 November 2026

Latest user feedback supersedes v005's elaborate limb construction and long performances. The user wants faster incidental physical acts, substantially less visual clutter, and a uniform style. Detail and minimalism are compatible. This is a design study, not a production behavior decision or an approved final character.

## What changes

- Keep the white pixel orb as the dominant silhouette and use the same face proportions, one-pixel contour and palette in every state.
- No limbs at rest. When necessary, use a filled hand nub attached directly to the body, projecting at most four source pixels. Feet are small contact marks, at most two pixels high, without shins or separate shoes.
- Retire the long outlined arms, visible joint chains, large shoes and sprawling rope construction from this direction. The earlier studies remain archived.
- Put detail into a clear intention, a small anticipatory pose, gaze, weight, one expressive action and a quick settle. Keep all rendering on the pixel grid.
- Use at most one restrained temporary prop. A small hat and side-facing glide can convey the moonwalk without a full costume or a long prop routine.
- Target ordinary physical acts around 0.8–1.6 seconds, the compact moonwalk at no more than 2.2 seconds. These are review targets, not scientifically established thresholds.
- Keep the agreed fixed-position three-pose idle, with each pose held for 500ms. Do not accelerate the resting cycle along with the tricks.

## Current experiment

v006 is a focused, coherent set: three-pose idle, greeting, backflip, clumsy flip, hop, stretch, attentive look, arrival, hat moonwalk and small-cape landing. Its six application states use the same drawing system. The previous 21-option catalogue is preserved in v005; it is not silently relabeled as having the new style.

The lead owns the shared drawing system, core acts and integration. The supporting artist owns only the two prop acts. The director reviews actual nominal-size frames and timing. The coordinator owns this brief, behavioral reasoning and final QA. Avoid parallel alternative limb systems within one batch.

## Why an engineer would want to communicate

The design hypothesis is that the companion feels approachable because it is responsive, legible and pleasant to return to. Playful acts establish personality, but their frequency and timing matter separately from their duration. Faster tricks alone do not demonstrate greater willingness to contribute.

Proposed behavioral rules for later integration:

| Situation | Proposed response | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A genuine idle gap | An occasional short flourish, then the quiet three-pose rest | A little independent personality without an ongoing performance |
| Engineer clicks, pastes or drops something | Interrupt the flourish immediately and adopt an attentive pose; open or accept input immediately | Their action takes priority over completing the trick |
| One useful question becomes available | One small pose change, then a static available cue; open the question on request | Make the invitation discoverable without repeated attention demands |
| Engineer answers Yes or No | The same brief warm acknowledgement; show the actual recorded answer in the panel | Welcome useful correction as much as confirmation |
| Engineer chooses unknown, Later or Dismiss | A neutral friendly settle, with no disappointment or repeated summons | Preserve voluntary collaboration and an easy exit |
| Something fails | A readable concerned/uncertain pose and a concrete explanation/retry action | Communicate the issue without implying the engineer hurt the character |

Do not use acrobatics to mark an overdue question or make the character sad when ignored. Do not imply that it sees the user's other apps or understands desktop activity unless a future authorized capability actually supplies that context. The visual mock only knows its own interaction events.

The random scheduler, cooldown values and how to detect an eligible idle gap remain proposals. v006 stays a manual audition with no autoplay. Input priority, stopping offscreen, reduced motion and meaningful stills remain part of the preview. No cross-application activity monitoring or native OS behavior is implemented.

Evaluate this direction with voluntary useful contributions, answer quality, correction ease, perceived interruption and desire to reopen the companion. Raw clicks or answer rate alone would not establish the intended experience.
